Why teens and parents struggle to find answers—and what we can do about it

Translation: Nearly 1 in 3 teens are struggling with depression. 1 in 4 have anxiety. Yet only about 1 in 3 are getting professional help. The crisis is real—and it's being underserved.
📊 What the Research Tells Us
Treatments That Work: We know evidence-based therapies work. CBT, DBT, mindfulness, medication when appropriate—these are proven interventions. The research is solid. The interventions are available.
Coping Skills Save Lives: Breathing exercises, grounding techniques, journaling, physical activity—teens who learn coping strategies show measurable improvements in mood, stress management, and crisis recovery.
Early Intervention Matters: When teens learn about their mental health early—when they understand what's normal and what isn't—they reach out sooner, get help faster, and recover better.
Parents Are Critical But Lost: Parents want to help their teens but don't know where to start. They don't understand the treatments. They're unsure how to talk about mental health. They feel helpless.
🔗 The Gap: Knowing vs. Doing
We know what works. But teens don't know how to find it. Parents don't know how to start. There's no bridge between the research and the living room conversation.

The barriers:

For Teens: "I know I'm struggling, but I don't know what it's called, what's normal, or what to do about it. I'm afraid of judgment. I don't know where to start."
For Parents: "I see my teen is suffering, but I don't understand depression vs. anxiety vs. normal teen moods. I don't know which treatments are evidence-based. I don't know how to talk about it without making it worse."
System-Wide: Crisis resources exist, but they're fragmented. Treatment options are scattered. No single place answers: "What's happening to me?" + "What do I do now?" + "How do I get help?"
