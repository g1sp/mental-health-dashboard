# 📋 Risk Factors & Causes Guide

## Overview

The **Risk Factors & Causes** tab (4th tab) helps you understand **why** youth experience mental health challenges. It explores 20 evidence-based risk factors across 7 different categories, sourced from trusted organizations like the CDC, NIMH, SAMHSA, and APA.

## What You'll Find in This Tab

- **Reset Filters Button** - Clear all filters and see all risk factors

### The 20 Risk Factors

#### Biological Factors (4)
These are physical and chemical factors that increase vulnerability:

1. **Genetic Predisposition** (30-40% genetic component)
   - Family history matters - if parents/siblings have mental illness, risk is higher
   - Warning Signs: Family history, multiple relatives affected
   - Prevention: Regular check-ups, early intervention, family communication

2. **Brain Chemistry Imbalances** (20-30% of youth with depression)
   - Neurotransmitter issues (serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine)
   - Warning Signs: Persistent low mood, loss of interest, sleep changes
   - Prevention: Regular sleep, exercise, omega-3s, stress reduction

3. **Hormonal Changes During Adolescence** (Peak age 13-18)
   - Puberty hormonal shifts increase vulnerability
   - Warning Signs: Mood swings, emotional sensitivity, social withdrawal
   - Prevention: Normalize discussion, monitor patterns, physical activity

4. **Sleep Disruption** (60% of teens get insufficient sleep)
   - Insufficient/disrupted sleep significantly increases risk
   - Warning Signs: Difficulty sleeping, daytime fatigue, irritability
   - Prevention: Consistent schedule, limit screens, dark cool environment

#### Environmental Factors (5)
These are life circumstances and situations that create stress:

5. **Academic Stress and Pressure** (40% report high stress)
   - Testing pressure and competitive school environments
   - Warning Signs: Test anxiety, perfectionism, school avoidance
   - Prevention: Study skills, realistic goals, time management, teacher communication

6. **Social Media and Cyberbullying** (59% experience cyberbullying)
   - Excessive social media use, social comparison, online bullying
   - Warning Signs: Anxiety without phone, comparison behaviors, sleep disruption
   - Prevention: Limit screen time, curate feeds, report bullying, balance online/offline

7. **Family Conflict and Instability** (40% affected by parental conflict)
   - Parental conflict, divorce, abuse, dysfunction
   - Warning Signs: Anxiety about home, behavioral changes, academic decline
   - Prevention: Family counseling, open communication, safe adult relationships

8. **Trauma and Adverse Experiences** (70% experience some trauma)
   - Past or ongoing trauma, abuse, neglect, significant losses
   - Warning Signs: Flashbacks, nightmares, hypervigilance, avoidance
   - Prevention: Trauma-focused therapy (TF-CBT), safety planning, support groups

9. **Economic Stress and Poverty** (40% of poor youth have conditions)
   - Financial instability and economic uncertainty
   - Warning Signs: Worry about basics, school absence, health neglect
   - Prevention: School support, community resources, mental health services

10. **Global Events and News Anxiety** (62% worried about future)
    - Climate anxiety, political stress, pandemic concerns
    - Warning Signs: Constant worry, news consumption, hopelessness
    - Prevention: Limit news, focus on controllable, community involvement

#### Social Factors (2)
These relate to relationships and social experiences:

11. **Peer Pressure and Social Isolation** (35% experience social anxiety)
    - Negative peer influence, social exclusion, friendship difficulties
    - Warning Signs: Social withdrawal, fear of judgment, social anxiety
    - Prevention: Build social skills, join activities, supportive friends

12. **Discrimination and Marginalization** (Disproportionate in marginalized communities)
    - Racism, discrimination, belonging to stigmatized groups
    - Warning Signs: Internalized shame, social withdrawal, hypervigilance
    - Prevention: Community connection, cultural affirmation, advocacy

#### Developmental Factors (3)
These relate to normal adolescent development that increases vulnerability:

13. **Developmental Brain Changes** (Normal for all teens)
    - Adolescent brain development affects emotion regulation
    - Warning Signs: Emotional reactivity, impulsivity, risk-taking
    - Prevention: Understanding development, structured environment, adult guidance

14. **Identity Formation Challenges** (LGBTQ+ youth 3-4x higher rates)
    - Struggles with sexual orientation, gender identity, cultural identity
    - Warning Signs: Identity confusion, social withdrawal, self-harm
    - Prevention: Affirming environment, LGBTQ+-competent therapy, community

15. **School Transitions and Major Changes** (Most youth experience some)
    - Moving, school changes, job loss, major life transitions
    - Warning Signs: Transition anxiety, academic decline, social difficulty
    - Prevention: Planning and preparation, maintain relationships, new routines

#### Behavioral Factors (1)
These are patterns of thought and behavior that increase risk:

16. **Perfectionism and High Self-Expectations** (40% of high-achievers)
    - Unrealistic self-expectations and perfectionism
    - Warning Signs: Self-criticism, anxiety over mistakes, all-or-nothing thinking
    - Prevention: Self-compassion, cognitive restructuring, realistic goals

#### Lifestyle Factors (2)
These are daily habits and choices that affect mental health:

17. **Lack of Physical Activity** (75% don't meet exercise guidelines)
    - Sedentary lifestyle increases risk; exercise is protective
    - Warning Signs: Sedentary behavior, low energy, poor mood
    - Prevention: Regular activity, team sports, outdoor time, gradual increase

18. **Chronic Medical Conditions** (2-3x higher disorder risk)
    - Youth with diabetes, asthma, etc. have higher mental health issues
    - Warning Signs: Denial of condition, non-adherence, low mood
    - Prevention: Integrated care, mental health support, peer support

#### Systemic Factors (1)
These are structural barriers in society:

19. **Limited Access to Mental Health Services** (75% don't receive treatment)
    - Lack of insurance, providers, or stigma prevents access
    - Warning Signs: Untreated symptoms, crisis escalation, self-harm
    - Prevention: School-based mental health, telehealth, community resources

20. **Substance Use and Experimentation** (25% with mental illness use substances)
    - Alcohol/drug use, often as self-medication, worsens mental health
    - Warning Signs: Increased use, behavioral changes, academic decline
    - Prevention: Education, alternatives to coping, trusted adults, counseling

## Important Concepts

### Risk vs. Cause
**Risk factors** increase the probability of a mental health condition, but they don't guarantee it will happen. Many youth with multiple risk factors remain mentally healthy due to protective factors.

**Causes** are more complex - mental health conditions usually result from the combination of multiple risk factors plus biology plus environment.

### Protective Factors
While this tab focuses on risk factors, **protective factors** reduce the impact:
- Strong relationships and support systems
- Coping and problem-solving skills
- Sense of purpose and meaning
- Access to mental health care
- Community belonging
- Resilience and adaptability

## When to Seek Help

### Contact a School Counselor If:
- You notice multiple warning signs in yourself or someone else
- A risk factor feels overwhelming
- You're not sure who to talk to
- You need someone to listen

### Contact a Mental Health Professional If:
- Symptoms last 2+ weeks
- They interfere with school/relationships/activities
- You're having thoughts of self-harm
- Family member is showing concern

### Call Crisis Line If:
- You're having suicidal thoughts
- You're in crisis
- You need immediate help
- **National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: 988**
- **Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741**
